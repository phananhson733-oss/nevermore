## What Is Outrank, and What Are You Actually Replacing?

**Outrank is a content automation platform that bundles three separate jobs — keyword research, article generation, and scheduled publishing to your CMS — behind one subscription.** Most people searching for an alternative only want one of the three replaced.

- **The published price is $99/month.** Its pricing page lists an All-in-One plan at $99/month or $999/year, covering 30 auto-published articles per month, automated keyword research, backlink exchange access, AI images and unlimited users (checked 17 August 2026).
- **Volume is an add-on, not a tier.** 60 articles/month adds $85, 90 adds $160. Human editing is a separate $1,399/month service, and multi-site accounts get automatic discounts of 10–20%.
- **The output is the product.** Thirty articles a month published on a schedule is the thing you are buying — not a research tool that happens to draft.
- **So "alternative" is ambiguous.** Replacing the research is a different purchase from replacing the writing, and both differ from replacing the publishing pipeline.

Work out which of the three you want before comparing anything, because the tools below are not priced on the same unit — and the budget end of that split is covered in [our guide to cheap SEO tools](/blog/best-cheap-seo-tools).

## Why It Matters Which Half You Are Replacing

Per-article cost is the number every roundup of Outrank alternatives leads with, and it is close to meaningless across these products because they do not meter the same thing — or bill on the same terms.

Outrank meters **articles**: 30 a month at $99 works out to $3.30 each. Arvow meters **credits**, listing Solo at $39/month billed annually or **$99/month month-to-month**, for 1,000 credits at roughly 10 credits per article. RightBlogger meters **sites and reports**: Solo at $59/month covers one site and 30 SEO reports, which is not an article count at all. Byword meters articles again, its pricing page metadata listing Starter at $99/month for 25 articles (all checked 17 August 2026).

Compare like billing with like and two of those four start at exactly the same $99. Arvow is still the cheapest per article on a month-to-month basis — about $0.99 against Outrank's $3.30 — but that is a hundredfold difference in *volume allowance*, not a discount on the same thing.

## How Outrank Alternatives Actually Differ in Real Agency Workflows

The useful split among Outrank alternatives is not by price tier. It is by which of the three jobs the tool refuses to do.

### Tools That Replace the Whole Pipeline

Arvow and Byword sit closest to Outrank. Arvow's tiers run Solo $39, Business $69 and Ultimate $249 per month when billed annually, against month-to-month rates of $99, $149 and $449; the annual discount is flagged "LIMITED TIME" and its summer sale applies to yearly plans only. Subscription credits reset monthly without rolling over, though separately purchased top-up credits do not expire. Byword's published tiers are Starter $99/month for 25 articles, Standard $299 for 80 and Scale $999 for 300.

These are like-for-like swaps. If Outrank's output volume is what you want and only the price or the writing quality bothers you, this is the row to shop.

### Tools That Replace Only Part of It

RightBlogger prices by site and by report rather than by article: Solo $59/month for one site and 30 SEO reports, Pro $89 for three sites and 90 reports, Agency $299 for ten or more sites and 300 reports. It carries a 7-day free trial and a 30-day money-back guarantee.

That is a different purchase. You are buying recurring site-level analysis, not a publishing schedule, and comparing its monthly price against Outrank's tells you nothing about whether either fits.

## Common Misreadings When Comparing These Tools

Four patterns cost people money when they shortlist Outrank alternatives:

1. **Reading an annual-commitment price as the monthly price.** Arvow's $39 Solo tier requires a twelve-month commitment; month-to-month it is $99, the same headline as Outrank. Any table that prints $39 beside $99 without a billing column is comparing different contracts.
2. **Treating credits as articles.** Credits also pay for images at roughly 3 each on Arvow, and articles over 18 headings cost double. A 1,000-credit month is a ceiling of about 100 articles, not a plan for 100.
3. **Assuming a bundled backlink feature is free of risk.** Outrank describes its Backlink Exchange as a network where you earn credits by hosting relevant backlinks and spend credits to receive them, verified automatically. RightBlogger also lists a Backlink Exchange, though its pricing page does not describe the mechanics. Google's spam policies name ["excessive link exchanges ('Link to me and I'll link to you') or partner pages exclusively for the sake of cross-linking"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) as link spam, and state that sites violating the policies "may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all." Whether a given implementation crosses that line depends on how it is used — the feature shipping inside a product does not make the practice safe, and the risk sits with your domain.
4. **Ignoring the volume question entirely.** The same policies define scaled content abuse as generating many pages "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users," and name using AI tools to produce many pages without adding value as an example. Google publishes no threshold, so volume triggers nothing by itself — but the more pages you ship, the more of your site rests on that judgement, whichever vendor produced them.

## How to Evaluate an Outrank Alternative

Three questions separate real Outrank alternatives from a lateral move. Work through them in this order:

1. **Which of the three jobs is failing?** If the drafts are the problem, a cheaper pipeline tool running a similar model changes nothing. If the publishing integration is the problem, that is a much narrower search.
2. **What does one month cost at your volume, on your billing term?** Convert every candidate to articles you will genuinely publish, include images, and price it month-to-month unless you are ready to commit for a year today.
3. **Who edits before publish?** Every vendor here, Outrank included, is a drafting system. Outrank sells human editing separately at $1,399/month, which is a fair signal of what that step costs when you do not do it yourself.

If none of the three has a clear answer, the honest conclusion is that a tool swap is not your bottleneck.

## How to Run a Two-Week Trial Step by Step

1. **Pick one real content brief you already planned to publish** — not a test topic. Vendor demos look identical on generic subjects.
2. **Run it through two candidates**, and count the editing minutes each draft needs before you would publish it. That number, not the subscription price, is the real cost difference.
3. **Check the publishing integration against your actual CMS**, including images, internal links and canonical handling.
4. **Turn any backlink-exchange feature off** for the trial, so you are evaluating the content on its own.
5. **Audit what you published** after two weeks — thin pages, duplicate angles, missing structured data. [Run our SEO agent over the site](https://gengrowth.ai/agents/seo) (Google sign-in required) and read the result before renewing anything.

Step five is the one people skip, and it is the one that tells you whether the volume helped. Automated output tends to fall down on internal linking first, which [how internal link structure moves authority](/blog/pagerank-sculpting) covers in more detail.

## Common Questions About Outrank Alternatives

**What is the cheapest Outrank alternative?**

On month-to-month billing, RightBlogger's $59 Solo tier is the lowest headline in this set, but it meters sites and reports rather than articles. For article volume, Arvow at $99 month-to-month gives roughly 100 articles against Outrank's 30 at the same price.

**Is there a free Outrank alternative?**

Byword lists a free start with 5 articles, and Outrank offers watermarked trial articles before you subscribe. Neither is a standing free plan at Outrank's thirty-a-month volume — free access in this category is trial-shaped.

**Do these tools actually publish to WordPress automatically?**

Outrank advertises native publishing integrations covering WordPress, Webflow, Shopify and several others. Confirm your specific CMS and check how each tool handles images and internal links before relying on it.

**Is 30 AI articles a month safe for SEO?**

Google's spam policies set no number. They judge whether pages exist primarily to manipulate rankings rather than help users, so volume raises the stakes of that judgement rather than triggering a threshold.

**Should I use a backlink exchange?**

Google names excessive link exchanges as link spam. A vendor bundling the feature does not change the policy, and the consequences land on your domain rather than theirs.

**Is Outrank worth $99 a month?**

If you will genuinely edit and publish 30 drafts a month, that is $3.30 per draft, below most freelance rates. If you publish four, you are paying about $25 each.

**What replaces only Outrank's keyword research?**

Dedicated keyword research platforms, not us — our agent audits what is already published rather than deciding what to write next. It is also the half where a wrong answer is cheapest to correct.
