<h2>Why This Correction Exists</h2>
<p>An earlier AstrologyWiki article on this site used a strong numerical narrative about user growth, timeline, revenue-style attribution, authority gains, and channel performance. That article read like a validated case study. The repository evidence available in this checkout does not support those outcome claims to publication standard, so the old framing needed to be corrected rather than quietly left online.</p>

<h2>What This Repository Can Verify</h2>
<p>There is one concrete public-site fact we can trace to a dated acceptance record in this repository. A 2026-07-31 public SEO audit acceptance for <code>astrologywiki.com</code> recorded that the bounded crawler in that run covered <strong>630 static pages discoverable to that crawler</strong>. That statement is narrow on purpose:</p>
<ul>
<li>It describes one crawler run, not a search engine index.</li>
<li>It does not prove total known URLs.</li>
<li>It does not prove search demand, traffic, users, revenue, or backlinks.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What We Cannot Verify from the Repository Evidence</h2>
<p>The current checkout does <strong>not</strong> contain reviewable source evidence for the following claims that previously appeared in the article:</p>
<ul>
<li>monthly active user totals or the headline growth outcome used in the older draft,</li>
<li>the accelerated timeline presented as a completed outcome,</li>
<li>revenue or paid-conversion attribution from content,</li>
<li>Domain Rating or other third-party authority metrics,</li>
<li>social performance totals such as engagement rates, impressions, or visit counts,</li>
<li>keyword-universe volume totals or ranking-count claims presented as established outcomes.</li>
</ul>
<p>That does not prove the numbers are false. It means this repository, by itself, cannot prove them. For a public case study, that distinction matters.</p>

<h2>What the Public Audit Boundary Actually Supports</h2>
<p>The relevant evidence in this repo is public-site and crawl-bounded. It supports statements about what a crawler could inspect on a public site at a given moment, plus the implementation boundaries of the marketing tooling around that crawl. It does not support product analytics, private search-console metrics, subscription outcomes, or cross-channel attribution unless those records are present and reviewable.</p>

<h2>What Teams Can Still Learn from the Corrected Record</h2>
<p>The useful lesson is not a specific user total. It is the standard for evidence honesty:</p>
<ol>
<li>Keep public-crawl facts separate from private growth metrics.</li>
<li>Do not relabel crawler coverage as search-engine indexing or ranking proof.</li>
<li>Do not imply attribution, revenue, or authority outcomes without reviewable source records.</li>
<li>When an older story overstates what the evidence can prove, publish a correction instead of letting the old claims keep ranking.</li>
</ol>
