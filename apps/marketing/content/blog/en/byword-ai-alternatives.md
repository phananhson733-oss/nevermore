## What Is Byword, and What Unit Does It Actually Sell?

**Byword is the one tool in this category whose tiers differ by article count and nothing else: $99/month for 25 articles, $299 for 80, $999 for 300 — $3.33 to $3.96 per article at every tier** (per its own pricing-page metadata, checked 24 August 2026). Everything else on a typical shortlist bundles other jobs into the price, which is why the headline numbers refuse to line up.

- **Its pricing-page metadata describes AI content generation; its homepage metadata adds the rest** — it researches keywords, writes in your brand voice using GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6 and Gemini 3.1 Pro, and publishes to WordPress, Webflow and four more CMSs.
- **The free start is 5 articles** — an evaluation denominated in the unit you would be buying: five real articles, not a timed trial.
- **There is no link network, visibility bundle or audit quota in the tier descriptions** — the three tiers differ by article count, and article count is nearly all they differ by.
- **Byword also maintains a programmatic SEO template library by vertical**, whose page describes "a value test that flags thin, spam-risk patterns before you build."

The wider budget market these tools sit in is mapped in [our guide to cheap SEO tools](/blog/best-cheap-seo-tools).

## Why It Matters Which Unit You Are Buying In

Put Byword's $99 next to the category's other $99s and the numbers describe different purchases.

Outrank's $99 buys 30 auto-published articles plus a backlink exchange. BabyLoveGrowth's $99 buys 30 articles plus a partner link network, AI-citation tracking and a Reddit agent. Arvow's $99 buys 1,000 credits, where a standard article costs about 10 credits — and long articles cost double. Autoblogging.ai enters at $19 for a credit allowance, not an article count. Frase's $49 buys 10 articles plus 50 audit pages inside an approval-gated loop.

Three of those purchases carry the same $99 sticker, and the five of them bill in five different units. A comparison that ignores the units is wrong before it starts, and this query's results include community threads where vendor arithmetic gets re-run in public.

Byword's own tiers are the baseline: $3.96 per article at Starter, $3.74 at Standard, $3.33 at Scale. Outrank sells a fixed 30-article bundle at $99 with publishing and its backlink exchange included — at $3.30 per article it undercuts every Byword tier on rate, and the extras are a bonus if you would use them and dead weight if you would not. ### If You Buy Credits Instead

Arvow's Solo plan is $99/month month-to-month ($39/month on a limited-time annual-commitment offer) for 1,000 credits; at its stated rate of about 10 credits per article that is roughly 100 standard articles, with the caveat that articles over 18 headings cost double credits. Autoblogging.ai runs from $19/month across six tiers, billed in credits with per-mode costs — the arithmetic is covered in [our autoblogging ai alternatives comparison](/blog/autoblogging-ai-alternatives). Credit systems reward short, standard articles and charge more for long ones — the surcharges sit in each vendor's stated credit rules — so convert your real article shape before trusting any per-article estimate.

BabyLoveGrowth bundles links, AI-citation tracking and a Reddit agent with its 30 articles — analyzed in [our babylovegrowth alternatives breakdown](/blog/babylovegrowth-alternatives). Frase couples 10 articles with 50 audit pages and a decay watcher at $49. If those surrounding jobs are what you actually wanted, Byword was never the right shelf.

## Common Misreadings When Comparing These Tools

1. **Dividing a credit price by an assumed article count.** Arvow's "about 10 credits per article" doubles past 18 headings; Autoblogging.ai bills credits across seven generation modes with no single published per-article rate. A per-article figure computed from someone else's credit system needs its assumptions attached, or it is guesswork dressed as arithmetic.
2. **Reading Byword's $999 tier as an enterprise plan.** It is the same unit at volume — 300 articles at $3.33 each — not a different product with added services. The tier descriptions in its page metadata differ by article count, not by feature set.
3. **Treating "publishes to your CMS" as equivalent everywhere.** Byword's metadata names WordPress, Webflow and four more CMSs; Scalenut lists auto-publish from its $89 tier for WordPress and Shopify; Frase publishes on approval. The verb is the same; the gate in front of it is not.
4. **Assuming the premium per article buys a named capability.** Byword's pages name its models and its CMS list, but the tier descriptions do not itemize what the $3.96 buys that a $1 credit article lacks. That is a question for the free 5-article start, not for a pricing table.

## How to Evaluate a Byword AI Alternative

Three questions, in order:

1. **What is your real monthly article volume and shape?** Under 25 standard articles, Byword's Starter and Arvow's monthly Solo land at the same $99 outlay; only Autoblogging.ai's $19 entry undercuts them, and whether its entry credits cover your volume depends on its mode costs. Long, heavily structured articles halve what a credit buys — the gap to per-article pricing narrows, though on these numbers it does not close.
2. **Would you use the bundle extras if they were free?** If links, visibility tracking or Reddit presence would go unused, a bundle discount is not a discount. If you would buy them anyway, the bundles beat any per-article rate.
3. **Who checks the output before it ships?** Every tool here can publish into your CMS. The cost that dominates at volume is editing minutes per article, and no pricing page states it.

If the honest answer to the first question is "five articles a month," start with Byword's free five and spend nothing this month.

## How to Run a Two-Week Trial Step by Step

1. **Use Byword's free 5-article start as the baseline** — same topics, same brand voice; its pages state simply "start free with 5 articles."
2. **Generate the same five topics in one credit tool** (Autoblogging.ai advertises free access without a card) and record the actual credits consumed per article, not the advertised rate.
3. **Count editing minutes per draft** before you would publish under your brand, and multiply by your monthly volume — that number usually dwarfs the subscription delta.
4. **Publish to a staging site, not production**, until you have read every draft end to end.
5. **Audit what you already have before buying volume.** [Run our SEO agent over the site](https://gengrowth.ai/agents/seo) — if existing pages are thin on structure, metadata or internal links, more articles amplify the problem. The diagnosis workflow is covered in [the pillar on reading your own Search Console data](/blog/striking-distance-keywords).

## Common Questions About Byword AI Alternatives

**What is the cheapest Byword alternative?**

Autoblogging.ai enters at $19/month, but it bills in credits, not articles — the honest comparison is credits consumed on your real article shape, measured in a trial.

**Is Outrank a direct replacement?**

At $99 for 30 articles with publishing included, it covers Byword's job and adds a backlink exchange. The exchange is a policy decision worth making separately — it is the part Byword's tier descriptions do not list, and one you may not want by default.

**Why is Byword more expensive per article than credit tools?**

Its pages name premium models (GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro) and multi-CMS publishing, but no pricing page settles a quality question. The free 5-article start against a credit tool's free tier is a same-week, zero-cost answer.

**Does Byword build backlinks or track AI visibility?**

Its tier descriptions list neither — the tiers differ by article count. If those jobs matter, the bundle category (Outrank, BabyLoveGrowth) or [our frase alternatives comparison](/blog/frase-alternatives) is the right shelf.

**Is there a free Byword alternative?**

Free entry points exist across the set — Byword's own 5 free articles, Autoblogging.ai's advertised free access — but no tool here lists a permanently free monthly plan. Treat free as an evaluation window.

**What about programmatic SEO at scale?**

Byword maintains a programmatic template library by vertical, and its own page warns it flags "thin, spam-risk patterns before you build." Whatever tool you pick, that warning applies: scale multiplies whatever quality you feed it.

**Do I need any of these if my traffic problem is not content volume?**

Then none of them address it. An audit of what is already published — metadata, heading structure, JSON-LD — is the cheaper first step, and it is the only job our agent does.
