## What Is RightBlogger, and What Does Its Meter Actually Count?

**RightBlogger is the one tool in this category whose primary meter is sites rather than output: every plan includes unlimited AI words, and its own pricing page says plans "scale with how many sites you run"** (checked 24 August 2026). Solo is $59/month billed monthly ($49/month billed yearly) for 1 connected site; Pro is $89 ($69 yearly) for 3 sites; Agency is $299 ($199 yearly) for 10 or more.

- **The real limits sit elsewhere**: 30, 90 or 300 SEO reports a month by tier; one, three or ten active automations (one per connected site); a scheduling queue holding up to 30 posts at once; and a ceiling of five published posts a day per site. Manual creation and publishing stay unmetered.
- **A Site Agent runs audits on connected sites** — weekly on Solo and Pro, daily on Agency.
- **A Backlink Exchange is listed at every tier** in the feature grid, with no description of how it works on the pricing page itself.
- **The free path is measured in words, not articles**: a free account carries 2,000 words a month with no card, and the 7-day trial is "$0 due today" with a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Everything else on a typical shortlist meters the output instead. Where the wider budget market sits is mapped in [our guide to cheap SEO tools](/blog/best-cheap-seo-tools).

## Why It Matters Which Meter You Hit First

Put RightBlogger's $59 next to the category's other entry prices and the numbers describe different constraints.

Byword's $99 buys 25 articles. Arvow's $99 buys 1,000 credits at about 10 per standard article. Autoblogging.ai's $19 buys a credit allowance. Outrank's $99 buys a fixed 30-article bundle with its backlink exchange included. Frase's $49 buys 10 articles plus 50 audit pages. RightBlogger's $59 buys one site with unlimited words — with the automated path bounded by one active automation and five published posts a day per site.

A single-site publisher pushing forty drafts a month blows past Frase's 10, Byword's 25 and Outrank's 30 while RightBlogger's word meter never moves — only Arvow's 1,000-credit allowance keeps pace, at the same $99. A five-site portfolio at three posts each inverts the math: five sites put you on RightBlogger's $299 Agency step, while the unit-priced tools reach five sites on cheaper tiers — Frase's Professional at $129 and Arvow's Business at $149 — for a total volume neither would strain.

## Common Misreadings When Comparing These Tools

1. **Reading "unlimited AI words" as an unlimited automated pipeline.** Its FAQ states the scheduling queue holds "up to 30 posts" at once and "each site publishes at most 5 per day," and Solo runs one active automation. Manual creation and publishing stay unlimited; the automated path is what carries the structured limits. Compare those, not the word claim.
2. **Comparing the yearly rate against competitors' monthly rates.** $49/$69/$199 are annual-commitment prices, billed annually as $588, $828 and $2,388; month-to-month is $59/$89/$299. Most vendors here run a two-rate structure — Byword's pricing lists monthly rates only, and Arvow's lower figure is a limited-time annual promotion — so fix the billing term for each candidate before building the table.
3. **Counting the Backlink Exchange as a link-building plan.** It appears at every tier as a feature label, and the pricing page does not describe how it works. However it operates in practice, evaluate it from the vendor's own documentation as a separate decision from the content subscription.
4. **Treating the SEO-report quota as unlimited too.** The word meter is off; the report meter is on — 30 a month at Solo. If audits are the half you use most, that quota is the number to compare, not the word claim.

## How to Evaluate a RightBlogger Alternative

Three questions, in order:

1. **Which meter did you max out last month — words, sites, reports, or daily publishing slots?** The answer picks your column. You will rarely hit all four; the one you do hit is the one worth paying for.
2. **What does your real publishing cadence cost on each meter?** Multiply your monthly drafts by your site count and price it three ways: per article, per credit, per site. The cheapest structure flips at surprisingly low volumes.
3. **Would you buy the extras on their own?** The backlink exchange, the audits, the social integrations — if a bundled feature would not survive as a separate purchase decision, it should not tip the comparison.

If the honest answer to the first question is "none of them," a free account at 2,000 words a month costs nothing while you find out.

## How to Run a Two-Week Trial Step by Step

1. **Start the 7-day trial with your real site connected** — the per-site structure only shows its value against your actual publishing cadence, not a test blog.
2. **Run one week at your true volume** and watch which meter moves: words will not, but reports, automation slots and the per-day publishing ceiling will.
3. **Price the same week on a per-article tool** — Byword's free 5-article start or a credit tool's trial — and compare what the week would have cost on each meter.
4. **Read the drafts end to end before anything auto-publishes**, and count editing minutes per draft; that number usually outweighs the subscription delta.

## Common Questions About RightBlogger Alternatives

**What is the cheapest RightBlogger alternative?**

Autoblogging.ai enters at $19/month on a credit meter. Whether it is actually cheaper depends on your volume — at high single-site volume, credits run out where unlimited words would not.

**Is Outrank a direct replacement?**

Closest in spirit: $99 for 30 auto-published articles with a backlink exchange. It meters a per-site article bundle — its pricing page gives volume discounts by site count — so a single high-volume site fits RightBlogger's structure better and predictable volume fits a capped bundle better.

**Does RightBlogger really have no usage limit?**

Its pricing page lists unlimited AI words on every plan, and its FAQ describes structured limits on the automated path: a queue holding up to 30 posts, at most five published a day per site, and one to ten active automations by tier. SEO reports are metered too. Unlimited describes manual generation and publishing, not the automations.

**Which alternative handles multiple sites cheapest?**

It depends which step you land on. RightBlogger's $89 Pro covers 3 sites and undercuts most unit-priced tiers that reach the same count — Frase's 5-site Professional is $129, Arvow's 5-site Business $149. At ten sites the comparison flips: $299 Agency against Frase's $239 Scale. Run the arithmetic on your own portfolio rather than assuming either direction.

**Is there a free RightBlogger alternative?**

RightBlogger's own free account is the floor and it recurs — 2,000 words every month, no card. Byword starts free with 5 articles and Autoblogging.ai advertises free access without a card; those read as one-time evaluation paths rather than recurring allowances.

**What about the Backlink Exchange?**

It is listed at every tier, and the pricing page does not describe how it works. As with any link program from any vendor in this table, read the vendor's own documentation and decide it as a question separate from the content subscription.

**Do I need any of these if my problem is not content volume?**

Then no meter here fixes it. An audit of what is already published — metadata, heading structure, JSON-LD — is the cheaper first step, and it is the only job our agent does.
